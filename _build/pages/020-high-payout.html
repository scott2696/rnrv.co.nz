What "high payout casino" actually means

The phrase gets used loosely enough to be meaningless, so here is the definition we use and rank against.

A high payout casino is one where the average return to player across the games you will realistically play is above the market norm, and the terms do not claw that advantage back. Three separate components, all of which have to hold:

  1. The catalogue's average RTP

    Not the best title in the lobby — the average across what is actually promoted and played. A site with one 99% game buried on page nine and a front page of 94% branded titles is not a high payout casino.

  2. The deployed configuration

    Studios ship the same game at several RTP levels and operators choose. Two sites can both carry Gorilla Mayhem and run it at 96.5% and 94.0% respectively. We check the info panel in each lobby rather than quoting the studio's headline figure.

  3. The terms that cap conversion

    A maximum conversion, a withdrawal cap or a heavy game weighting can make a high-RTP catalogue pay worse in practice than a lower one. RTP is theoretical; the cashier is real.

The arithmetic, once

RTP applies to turnover, not to deposits. If you deposit NZ$100 and play it through ten times — which happens quickly at NZ$0.40 a spin — your turnover is NZ$1,000. At 96% RTP your expected loss is NZ$40, or 40% of your deposit. At 94% it is NZ$60. That two-point difference is 50% more expensive for the identical session, and it is why catalogue RTP matters more than most players assume.

The trap in that table

Notice that the best-RTP games are exactly the ones weighted at 0–10% for bonus wagering. That is deliberate. If you claim a NZ$100 bonus at 40x and try to clear it on blackjack weighted at 10%, your NZ$4,000 requirement becomes NZ$40,000 of turnover. Clearing a bonus and maximising RTP are opposing goals — pick one per session and know which you are doing.

How we measured catalogue RTP

The ranking above is not built from operator claims. For each site:

  • We sampled 60 titles per lobby — the 30 most prominently placed on the homepage and category pages, plus 30 drawn at random from the full catalogue, so that a site cannot score well by burying good games where nobody finds them.
  • We opened each game and read the RTP from its information panel in that operator's lobby, capturing the deployed configuration rather than the studio's headline figure.
  • We weighted the prominent titles at 2× the random sample, because placement is what most players actually play.
  • We then applied a deduction for maximum conversion caps, withdrawal fees and heavy game weighting, since all three reduce realised return.

The spread across the 38 sites we assessed was narrower than the marketing suggests: roughly 95.1% at the bottom to 96.4% at the top. That 1.3-point spread is worth about NZ$130 per NZ$10,000 of turnover — real money for a regular player, and irrelevant for someone playing NZ$50 a month. Full methodology on our how we review page.

The comparison that actually matters in New Zealand

Forget which offshore casino is 0.4 points better than another for a moment. The consequential number is the gap between online and the machines down the road.

Class 4 gaming machines — pub and club pokies under the Gambling Act 2003 — return roughly 85–90%. Online pokies at the sites above return 95–97%. On identical turnover, the pub machine costs you around three times as much.

The reason is structural. A Class 4 machine funds a venue, a machine lease, staffing and a mandated community grant stream from the same turnover. An online operator carries none of those costs and competes in a market where RTP is published and directly comparable. Neither arrangement is a scandal — the Class 4 surplus goes to community grants by design — but a player should know which one buys more playing time.

Getting the best return in practice

  1. Decide whether this is a bonus session or an RTP session

    They are incompatible. Clearing a bonus means playing 100%-weighted pokies at capped stakes. Maximising return means playing blackjack or high-RTP pokies with no bonus attached. Mixing them produces the worst of both.

  2. Check the info panel, every time

    Ten seconds. The deployed RTP is frequently not the one in the review, and it can change between visits when an operator swaps configuration.

  3. Prefer European or French roulette over American

    Same game, 97.3% or 98.65% versus 94.74%. The double-zero wheel exists purely to take more, and it sits in most lobbies next to the single-zero one at the same table limits.

  4. Learn blackjack basic strategy if you play blackjack

    It is a printable chart, it takes an evening, and it is worth several percentage points of RTP. Playing blackjack on instinct gives away most of the advantage that makes it the best game in the lobby.

  5. Skip progressives unless you accept the trade

    Base RTP of 88–92% is the price of the jackpot. Fine as an occasional small-budget flutter, poor as a main game.

  6. Read the conversion cap before you deposit

    A high-RTP catalogue behind a 3× conversion cap pays worse than a mediocre one without. The cap is in the terms; find it.
