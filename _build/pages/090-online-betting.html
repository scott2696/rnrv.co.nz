The legal position on online betting in New Zealand, in full

New Zealand's betting and casino regimes are moving in opposite directions at the same time, which is why so much of what you read on this topic is wrong.

Betting: closing down

The Racing Industry Amendment Act 2025 received Royal Assent on 27 June 2025. It establishes that no person other than TAB NZ may offer racing betting or sports betting to New Zealanders, and it strengthens the enforcement powers available against operators that do.

The policy rationale is funding. Betting turnover is what pays for the New Zealand racing industry and for grants to national sporting organisations. Money bet with an offshore bookmaker left that system entirely, and the offshore betting charge introduced earlier recovered only part of it. The amendment closes the gap by making TAB NZ's position exclusive rather than merely advantaged.

Online casino: opening up

At the same time, the Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 came into force on 1 May 2026 and creates New Zealand's first licensing regime for online casino gambling. The Department of Internal Affairs is awarding up to 15 licences through a competitive auction, and from 1 December 2026 only operators with a licence or a pending application may serve New Zealanders. Our NZ online casino law page covers that timeline in detail.

So: casino is becoming legal and regulated domestically, while betting is becoming exclusively TAB NZ's. An operator offering both to New Zealanders faces two entirely different legal positions on the two halves of its own product.

What this actually means for your betting account

The offence sits with the operator, not with you. But an operator acting outside a legal framework produces consequences you feel, and you should weigh them honestly:

  • No New Zealand recourse. If an offshore book voids a winning bet or closes your account with a balance, there is no New Zealand regulator to escalate to. Your options are the offshore licensing authority, a public complaints forum, or nothing.
  • No fund segregation. Curaçao and Anjouan licensees are not required to hold customer money separately from operating money. TAB NZ operates under a completely different framework.
  • Payment friction can appear without warning. New Zealand banks periodically restrict transactions to offshore gambling operators. A deposit that worked last month may be declined this month, and a pending withdrawal can be caught in the middle.
  • Service can end abruptly. An operator that decides New Zealand is no longer worth the regulatory risk may close the market with limited notice. Withdraw balances rather than leaving them.
  • Nothing funds New Zealand sport. TAB NZ's surplus goes to racing and to national sporting organisations. A bet placed offshore contributes nothing to either. Whether that matters to you is your call, but it is a real difference and it is rarely mentioned.

How to read a price properly

Decimal odds and implied probability

New Zealand and Australian books use decimal odds, which show your total return per dollar staked including the stake. NZ$10 at 2.50 returns NZ$25: NZ$15 profit plus your NZ$10.

The useful move is converting a price to implied probability by dividing 1 by the odds:

Calculating the overround yourself

Take a two-way market. Warriors 1.85, Storm 2.00. Implied probabilities: 1÷1.85 = 54.1%, 1÷2.00 = 50.0%. Total 104.1%. The book's margin on that market is 4.1%.

Do this on three or four markets at any book and you will know within two minutes whether it is worth your turnover — which is a more useful skill than any tipping service.

Betting markets explained

Head to head / moneyline

Which team or competitor wins. The simplest market and almost always the cheapest in margin terms. Where a beginner should stay until the rest is understood.

Line / handicap

One side starts with a points advantage, evening the market. Warriors −6.5 means they must win by seven or more. Standard in NRL and rugby where mismatches are common.

Totals (over/under)

Whether combined score finishes above or below a set number. Independent of who wins, so it suits a view about tempo or conditions rather than about the result.

Futures / outrights

Season-long markets — NRL premiership, Super Rugby title, Melbourne Cup. Your money is tied up for months and the margin is usually wide.

Multis and same-game multis

Several selections combined, all of which must win. The margin compounds per leg — four legs at 104% is roughly 17% total. The most profitable product a book sells, by a distance.

Live / in-play

Betting during the event, with prices updating continuously. Margins are typically wider than pre-match and the delay between your click and acceptance works in the book's favour.

Player props

Individual performance — first try-scorer, runs scored, points total. Wide margins, and the market most likely to be limited if you win consistently.

Tote vs fixed odds (racing)

Fixed odds lock your price at placement. Tote pools all bets and pays a dividend determined after the race. TAB NZ offers both; offshore books almost always fixed odds only.

Two delays specific to betting

  • Market settlement. A winning bet is not a withdrawable balance until the market settles. Main markets settle within minutes of full time; some player props and futures take days or months. Check what is still unsettled before assuming a withdrawal is being held.
  • Integrity review. Books run automated checks for unusual patterns — a large stake on an obscure market, a sudden change in betting behaviour, correlated bets across accounts. A flagged bet goes to manual review, which is legitimate but slow. Betting on low-liquidity markets makes it more likely.

The free bet rule most people get wrong

A free bet returns winnings but not the stake. A NZ$100 free bet at 2.00 returns NZ$100, not NZ$200. At 5.00 it returns NZ$400. Because you never get the stake back regardless, the longer the price, the more the free bet is worth — the opposite of how most people use them. Do not put a free bet on a 1.30 favourite.

Placing your first bet

  1. Decide who you want holding your money

    TAB NZ if you want a New Zealand-regulated operator with New Zealand recourse. An offshore book if you have read the section above and accepted the trade-offs. This is the decision that matters; everything after it is detail.

  2. Check the currency and the margin before the bonus

    Confirm you can hold NZD. Then sample three markets you would actually bet and calculate the overround. Two minutes of arithmetic is worth more than any review, including this one.

  3. Register and verify immediately

    ID, proof of address, proof of payment method. Betting books run integrity checks as well as AML checks, so unverified accounts stall more often here than at casino sites.

  4. Set a deposit limit

    Before your first deposit, not after your first bad week. Every book on this page has them in account settings.

  5. Start with head-to-head on a sport you follow

    Your edge, if you have one, comes from knowing something the price does not reflect. That is only possible in a competition you actually watch. Skip multis entirely for the first month.

  6. Record every bet

    Date, market, stake, price, result. A spreadsheet tells you within two months whether you are actually up — and memory reliably says yes when the record says no. This is the single habit that separates bettors who know their position from those who do not.

Mistakes that cost New Zealand bettors money

  1. Betting multis because they feel like value

    Margin compounds per leg. A four-leg multi at 104% a leg carries about 17% total margin against you. They are the single most profitable product a bookmaker offers and that is not an accident.

  2. Shopping for bonuses instead of prices

    A NZ$350 free bet is worth about NZ$245 in practice, once. A three-point margin difference costs a regular bettor more than that every year, forever.

  3. Betting New Zealand racing with an offshore book

    117–125% overround against TAB NZ's pricing on the code it specialises in. There is no version of this that favours you.

  4. Chasing losses with in-play

    In-play betting is fast, emotional and carries wider margins than pre-match. It is where a controlled Saturday becomes an uncontrolled one.

  5. Leaving a balance in the account

    Offshore, unsegregated and one policy change away from being inaccessible. Withdraw.

  6. Not reading the free bet terms

    Minimum odds, excluded markets, expiry windows, stake-not-returned. All of it is in the terms and all of it changes the value.

Trust, safety and what to do when something goes wrong

Before you deposit

  • Confirm the licence number resolves on the regulator's own register, not just a footer badge.
  • Find the withdrawal caps and fees in the terms. If you cannot find them without asking support, that is a deliberate design choice.
  • Check whether your preferred market is excluded from bonus qualification.
  • Test live chat with a specific question — "what is your maximum weekly withdrawal?" — and keep the transcript.

If a bet is voided or a withdrawal is refused

  1. Get it in writing

    Email, not chat. Include account ID, bet reference, stake, price and the specific term you believe has been misapplied. Ask for a written decision and a complaints reference.

  2. Escalate internally

    Ask explicitly for the complaints or compliance team and for a decision deadline.

  3. Go to the licensing authority or ADR

    Named in the terms. Attach the written decision. Expect this to be slower and less predictable than a regulated-market complaint.

  4. Use a public mediator

    AskGamblers and Casino Guru run complaint services that operators respond to because unresolved public complaints damage acquisition. Often the most effective route.

  5. Understand the limits

    The Department of Internal Affairs is the right place to report an operator marketing illegally into New Zealand. It is not a route to recover a private balance from an offshore company.
