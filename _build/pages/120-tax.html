The short answer

If you are a recreational gambler in New Zealand, you pay no tax on your winnings and you declare nothing. Pokies, casino games, online casino, sports betting, racing, Lotto — all of it. Any amount. Inland Revenue treats gambling winnings as the proceeds of chance, not as income.

Two exceptions exist: gambling carried on as a business, and the disposal of cryptocurrency. Both are set out below.

Why New Zealand does not tax gambling winnings

New Zealand's income tax system taxes income, and income has a settled meaning: something that comes in with sufficient regularity and from a source that produces it. A windfall — a lottery win, a found sum, a gift, a gambling win — is a receipt of capital in nature rather than income.

Gambling winnings are treated as the proceeds of chance. The player is not carrying on a business, is not providing a service, and has no reasonable expectation of profit. Over any sufficient run, the player loses; the occasional win is a chance outcome, not a return on an activity.

The logic runs both ways, which is why it is stable: because winnings are not income, losses are not deductible. If the state taxed wins, it would face claims for the far larger aggregate of losses.

The two exceptions

1. Gambling as a business

If gambling amounts to a business carried on for profit — with system, scale, regularity and a realistic expectation of profit — the proceeds can be taxable as business income. A high bar, and one that catches very few people. Full test below.

2. Cryptoasset disposals

The gambling win itself is untaxed. But Inland Revenue treats crypto as property, so disposing of coins you won — selling, swapping, or spending them — can create a taxable event on any gain in value since you received them. Full explanation below.

The professional gambler test

There is no single statutory test and no bright line. Inland Revenue and the courts assess the overall character of the activity, weighing a number of factors together.

Factors considered in assessing whether gambling amounts to a business. No single factor is decisive; the assessment is of the pattern as a whole.
FactorPoints towards recreationalPoints towards business
ScaleModest stakes relative to incomeTurnover that would be commercial in any other context
FrequencyOccasional or weeklyDaily, sustained over years
Time devotedEvenings and weekendsFull-time, displacing other work
System or skillReliance on chance; games with no available edgeAn identifiable, applied edge — modelling, arbitrage, advantage play
Record-keepingNone, or casualDetailed, business-like, used to manage the activity
Reliance on the incomeOther employment or income supports youGambling is the main or only income source
Expectation of profitPlaying for entertainment; expecting to lose overallA genuine, evidenced expectation of profit over time
OrganisationPersonal accounts, informalDedicated bankroll, staking arrangements, possibly an entity

Where this line actually falls

The game matters enormously. In pokies, roulette or any RNG casino game, no edge is mathematically available — the house edge is built into the paytable and cannot be overcome by skill. It is very difficult to argue a business exists in an activity with a guaranteed negative expected value.

Poker is different, because it is played against other players rather than against the house, and a skilled player can have a genuine long-run edge. That is why the small number of New Zealanders who have faced this question have generally been poker players.

Sports betting sits in between: an edge is theoretically available, but bookmakers limit or close accounts that demonstrate one, which tends to prevent the scale a business requires.

If several rows in the table above describe you, take advice. A New Zealand chartered accountant will resolve it in one conversation, and the cost of getting it wrong in either direction is higher than the cost of asking.

Cryptocurrency winnings: the exception most people miss

This section exists because we could not find a single competitor page in the New Zealand market that covers it, and it applies to a growing number of players as crypto cashiers become the default fast-withdrawal route.

The two questions, separated

Question one: is the win taxable? No. A gambling win is the proceeds of chance whether it arrives as New Zealand dollars or as USDT.

Question two: is the crypto disposal taxable? Possibly. Inland Revenue treats cryptoassets as property, not currency. Disposing of property can produce a taxable gain. Disposal includes:

  • Selling crypto for New Zealand dollars
  • Swapping one cryptoasset for another
  • Using crypto to pay for goods or services
  • Gifting it in some circumstances

Use a stablecoin. Winning and withdrawing in USDT means the asset does not move against the dollar, so there is essentially no gain to account for on disposal. What happens after you win

The win is untaxed. What you do with the money afterwards may not be.

  • Interest on the winnings in a savings account or term deposit is taxable income, with resident withholding tax deducted at source. Give your bank your IRD number or you will be taxed at the no-declaration rate.
  • Dividends from shares bought with the winnings are taxable income.
  • Rental income from a property bought with the winnings is taxable income, with the usual deduction rules.
  • Foreign investment may bring you within the foreign investment fund rules depending on the amount, which is worth checking above roughly NZ$50,000 of overseas holdings.
  • Gifting the money attracts no gift duty — gift duty was abolished in New Zealand in 2011 — though very large gifts can have implications for residential care subsidies and relationship property.

None of this makes the win taxable. It simply means a large win moves you into ordinary investment taxation, which is worth a conversation with an accountant if the sum is significant.

How New Zealand compares

CountryTax on player winningsNotes

IrelandNoneBetting duty falls on the bookmaker.

The common design across most of these countries is the same: tax the operator rather than the player. It is simpler to administer, it captures revenue at the point of profit, and it avoids the losses-deduction problem entirely. New Zealand's new online casino licensing regime follows that pattern.

Records worth keeping

If you are clearly recreational

Nothing is required for tax. Keeping a simple record of deposits and withdrawals is still worthwhile — for managing your own spending, not for Inland Revenue. Most people materially underestimate what they have staked over a year.

If you won in cryptocurrency

Date, coin, quantity and NZD value at receipt, and the same at disposal. This is the only record that determines whether a gain exists. Capture the receipt side yourself — casinos do not provide it.

If you might be near the professional line

Everything. Every stake, every result, time devoted, methodology, and any staking arrangements. Contemporaneous records are the only useful evidence if the question is ever asked, and reconstructed ones are not.
