How authorship works here

Every page is signed

A named writer, a named fact-checker, and the date the page was last reviewed. No anonymous content, no operator-supplied copy, no house byline hiding who did the work.

Nothing publishes unchecked

Claire writes, Elizabeth checks. Two people on every page, every time — including the short ones.

Testing is first-hand

Real accounts, real New Zealand dollars, real withdrawals, timed to the minute. Never an operator's press kit.

Dates are honest

The review date at the foot of a page is when someone actually re-checked it, not when the file was last touched. If a page has not been reviewed recently, you can see that.

Corrections are attributed

When we get something wrong we fix it, note the correction and its date at the foot of the page, and update the review date. We do not silently edit.

Conflicts are declared

RNRV earns affiliate commission, disclosed on every page carrying a link. Neither author holds a personal financial interest in any operator beyond that site-wide relationship.

Why this page exists

Search engines call it E-E-A-T — experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trustworthiness — and it is a reasonable framework, but the underlying point is older and simpler than the acronym.

If a page tells you a casino paid a withdrawal in two hours, that claim is worth something only if you know who tested it, when, on what payment rail, and whether they had any reason to overstate it. If a page tells you the wagering requirement is 40x, it is worth something only if someone actually opened the terms. And if a page tells you what the Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 requires, it is worth something only if the person writing it can read legislation.

Most casino affiliate content answers none of those questions, because most of it is assembled from operator marketing by people who have never opened an account. This page, the methodology page and the byline on every article are how we make it possible for you to check us.

Including, we hope, checking us when we are wrong. The contact page goes to real people.
