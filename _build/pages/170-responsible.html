The honest arithmetic

We write about gambling and earn money from it, which makes it more important to state this clearly rather than less.

Every game on this site has a negative expected value

Each one returns less than it takes. That is the design, not a flaw, and it is how the whole industry is funded. Our RTP tables show exactly how much less for each game type: about 4% of turnover on a typical pokie, 0.7% on live blackjack played correctly, 13% on a New Zealand pub pokie.

A higher RTP makes the entertainment cheaper. It does not make it profitable. No system, staking plan, bet-sizing method or pattern-reading changes that, and anyone selling one is selling you something that cannot work.

Warning signs worth taking seriously

Gambling harm rarely arrives suddenly. It usually looks like a series of small, individually explicable decisions.

In your behaviour

  • Gambling with money needed for rent, bills or food
  • Chasing losses — depositing again to win back what you just lost
  • Borrowing to gamble: credit cards, buy-now-pay-later, loans, family
  • Gambling longer or for more than you planned, repeatedly
  • Betting larger amounts to get the same feeling
  • Gambling to escape stress, low mood or boredom

In how you feel and what you hide

  • Lying about how much or how often you play
  • Hiding statements, or gambling when nobody is around
  • Irritability or anxiety when you try to cut down
  • Losing interest in things you used to enjoy
  • Relief rather than enjoyment when you play
  • Guilt or shame afterwards, consistently

Two or more of these is a reason to make one free call. 0800 654 655. It is not a commitment to stop and it is not a diagnosis — it is a conversation with someone who has had it thousands of times before.

Tools you can switch on in five minutes

Every operator we list has these in account settings, usually under Responsible Gambling or Account Limits. If a site does not have them, it does not appear on RNRV — that is a hard gate in our scoring.

  1. Deposit limits

    Daily, weekly or monthly caps on what you can put in. Tightening usually takes effect immediately; loosening takes a cooling-off period of 24 hours to 7 days. Set one when you open the account, not when you need it.

  2. Loss limits

    A cap on net losses over a period, which is a better control than a deposit limit because it accounts for money you win and then play back.

  3. Session time limits and reminders

    A pop-up at intervals showing elapsed time and net position, or a hard session cut-off. Live tables and autoplay both make time pass faster than you think.

  4. Time-out

    A short cooling-off period, typically 24 hours to 6 weeks, after which the account reopens automatically. The right tool for a bad week. Using one is not an admission of anything.

  5. Self-exclusion

    6 months, 1 year, 5 years or permanent. The operator must close your access and stop marketing to you, and it cannot be reversed early. Do it at every account you hold, because offshore self-exclusion does not carry across operators.

  6. Reality checks on spend

    Most cashiers show deposit and withdrawal history. Look at the twelve-month total rather than the last session. Almost everyone underestimates it, often by a large multiple.

Bank gambling blocks — the most effective tool here

Why this one works when others do not

A gambling block at your bank declines transactions to merchants coded as gambling. It works across every operator at once, rather than one account at a time, and it does not depend on an offshore company honouring a request it is not legally obliged to honour.

Most implementations include a cooling-off period of 48 hours or more before the block can be lifted — deliberate friction at exactly the moment friction helps most.

Several New Zealand banks offer this. Ask yours, through internet banking or by phone. It takes minutes.

Related and worth knowing: do not gamble on credit. Your card issuer almost always codes a gambling deposit as a cash advance, so interest starts immediately at 20%+ with no grace period, plus a 2–3% fee. Beyond the cost, depositing borrowed money is one of the clearest warning signs on this page. Our payment methods page covers this in detail.

Blocking software

BetBlocker

Free, charity-run, blocks tens of thousands of gambling sites across your devices. Once a restriction period is set it cannot be removed early. No cost and no catch.

Gamban

Paid software blocking gambling sites and apps across devices. Widely used and often supplied free through treatment services — ask when you call.

Device and network controls

Router-level filtering, and the parental controls built into iOS, Android, Windows and macOS, can block gambling categories across a whole household.

Blocking software works best combined with a bank block. Software stops the easy route; the bank block stops the workaround.

If you are worried about someone else

Living with someone else's gambling is hard, and every service on this page supports you directly. You do not need the person gambling to make the call, and you do not need their permission.

What helps

  • Talking about the impact on you, rather than accusing
  • Picking a calm moment, not the immediate aftermath of a loss
  • Protecting shared finances — separate accounts, moving savings
  • Encouraging one free call rather than demanding they stop
  • Getting support for yourself, whether or not they get support
  • Being patient. Change is rarely a single decision.

What usually does not

  • Paying their gambling debts — removes the consequence, not the cause
  • Ultimatums you are not prepared to follow through on
  • Monitoring and policing, which tends to drive it underground
  • Taking responsibility for their recovery
  • Keeping it secret from everyone, including your own support network
  • Waiting for them to reach a "rock bottom"

Gambling Helpline 0800 654 655 and Problem Gambling Foundation 0800 664 262 both provide free whānau support. Call for yourself.

Myths that cost New Zealanders money

Common beliefs, and what is actually true.
The beliefThe reality
"This machine is due for a payout"Every spin is independent. 200 losing spins do not change the probability on spin 201. This is the gambler's fallacy and it is the most expensive belief in the room.
"I can win it back"Chasing increases the amount at risk without improving the odds. The expected value of the next bet is the same negative number it always was.
"I'm on a lucky streak"Streaks are how randomness looks from inside. They are visible only in hindsight and predict nothing.
"I've nearly won — two out of three symbols"A near miss is a design feature engineered to feel like almost-winning. It is a loss, and it is deliberately built to not feel like one.
"Betting more gets me out faster"Larger stakes increase variance in both directions and increase the expected loss per hour. It changes speed, not direction.
"Higher RTP means I'll win"All RTP under 100% is a loss over a long run. Higher RTP means a smaller loss, not a profit.
"Bonuses are free money"A bonus is a contract with a wagering requirement, a maximum bet, a weighting table and often a conversion cap. See our bonus analysis for what each one actually costs to clear.
"The casino can see my bets and adjusts the game"At a licensed operator running certified software, no. The edge is in the paytable and is disclosed as RTP — there is nothing to gain from interfering, and everything to lose.

Our position

RNRV earns affiliate commission from casino operators. Given that, here is what we hold ourselves to on this subject.

  • Every page carries helpline information, not only this one.
  • An operator without deposit limits, loss limits, time-outs and self-exclusion is not listed here. It is a hard gate, not a scored criterion.
  • We publish the arithmetic even when it is inconvenient. The negative expected value table above, the no-deposit bonus valuation showing typical value is NZ$1–4, and the repeated point that a better RTP is still a loss.
  • We tell readers when to decline a bonus. Our bonus page has a section on it, because a bonus you cannot clear is a restriction on your own money.
  • No urgency tactics. No countdown timers, no fake scarcity, no "expires in 10 minutes". Those exist to bypass judgement.
  • Nothing designed to appeal to under-18s. No cartoon styling, no gaming-crossover framing.
  • We name the harm. Gambling causes serious harm to a minority of people and to the people around them. That is not a footnote on this site.

If you think we have fallen short of any of that on a particular page, tell us through the contact page. It is the kind of message we most want to receive.
