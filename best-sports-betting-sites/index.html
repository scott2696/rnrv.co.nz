The legal position in full

The Racing Industry Amendment Act 2025 received Royal Assent on 27 June 2025. It establishes that no person other than TAB NZ may offer racing betting or sports betting to New Zealanders, and strengthens the enforcement powers available against operators that do.

The rationale is funding. Betting turnover pays for the New Zealand racing industry and for grants to national sporting organisations. A bet placed with an offshore bookmaker contributed nothing beyond a limited offshore charge, and the amendment closes that gap by making TAB NZ's position exclusive.

What follows for you

  • You commit no offence by betting offshore. The obligation is on the operator.
  • You have no New Zealand recourse. A voided bet or a closed account with a balance cannot be escalated to a New Zealand regulator.
  • Your funds are not segregated. Curaçao and Anjouan licensees are not required to hold customer money separately from operating money.
  • Payment access can change without notice. New Zealand banks periodically restrict transactions to offshore gambling merchants.
  • An operator can exit the market abruptly. Withdraw balances rather than leaving them sitting.
  • Nothing funds New Zealand sport. TAB NZ's surplus goes to racing and to national sporting organisations; an offshore bet contributes to neither.

Said plainly

If you want a lawful, New Zealand-regulated bookmaker with New Zealand consumer protection, use TAB NZ or Betcha. We earn nothing from that recommendation and make it anyway. The rest of this page exists because many New Zealanders use offshore books regardless, and they are better served by accurate margin data than by a page that pretends the legal question does not exist.

The free bet rule

A free bet returns winnings but not the stake. NZ$100 at 2.00 returns NZ$100; NZ$100 at 5.00 returns NZ$400. Since you never get the stake back either way, a free bet is worth more on a longer price. Most people do the opposite and put it on a short-priced favourite, which wastes most of its value.

  1. Settle the legal question first

    TAB NZ if you want a lawful, regulated, New Zealand operator. An offshore book if you have read the trade-offs and accepted them. Everything else is detail.

  2. Check the account currency

    NZD or you are paying 2–3% each way on conversion, which exceeds most books' margin.

  3. Sample three markets and calculate the overround

    1 ÷ decimal odds for each outcome, sum them. Two minutes per market. This tells you more than any review.

  4. Find the withdrawal caps in the terms

    Not by asking support. If they are not findable, that is information in itself.

  5. Check depth in the codes you actually bet

    Open your sport and count the markets on one fixture. Four markets and forty markets are very different products.

    And run the turnover arithmetic on it before it influences the decision.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best sports betting site for New Zealanders?

On measured margin, Rooster Bet — a 103.4% overround on NRL head-to-head, the tightest of any book we sampled, and the best Super Rugby pricing too. It also runs casino and sportsbook on one wallet, which suits a lot of New Zealand accounts.

The honest caveat is in the legal section: none of these is lawfully permitted to take a New Zealand bet. TAB NZ is.

Is sports betting legal in New Zealand?

For you, yes — there is no offence and no penalty for a New Zealander who places a bet online.

For the operator, no, unless it is TAB NZ. Under the Racing Industry Amendment Act 2025 (Royal Assent 27 June 2025), TAB NZ holds the sole legal right to offer racing and sports betting to New Zealanders, and it is an offence for an offshore bookmaker to accept New Zealand bets. Full detail on our online betting NZ page.

What is overround and why does it matter more than a bonus?

Overround is the bookmaker's built-in margin. Convert each price in a market to implied probability (1 ÷ decimal odds) and add them: a fair book totals 100%, and everything above is the cut.

On a two-way NRL market, 102% is excellent, 104% normal, 108% expensive. That spread is roughly 3% of every dollar you turn over. Bet NZ$200 a week for a year and a three-point margin difference costs about NZ$310 — more than any welcome bonus on this page, and it recurs every year.

Which sportsbook has the best NRL odds for Kiwis?

Rooster Bet at 103.4% on head-to-head in our September 2026 sample, followed by Bet&Play at 104.2%. Both price the NRL as a primary market rather than an afterthought, which shows in the line and totals markets too.

TAB NZ is also genuinely competitive on NRL, and it offers the local market depth — first try-scorer on New Zealand-relevant players, NZ Warriors specials — that offshore books do not carry.

Where should I bet on New Zealand racing?

TAB NZ, without qualification. Every offshore book in our sample priced New Zealand thoroughbred win markets at 117% to 125% overround, which is enormously wide, because none of them holds real liability on a Thursday card at Te Rapa or Riccarton.

TAB NZ also offers tote betting alongside fixed odds, which no offshore book here does. For New Zealand racing specifically there is no version of the comparison that favours an offshore operator.

What is the difference between a free bet and a deposit match?

A deposit match credits bonus funds you must turn over before withdrawing. A free bet gives you a stake you did not fund, but returns winnings only — a NZ$100 free bet at 3.00 returns NZ$200, not NZ$300.

Because the stake is never returned, a free bet is worth roughly 65–75% of its face value, and it is worth more on longer odds than short ones. Put a free bet on a 5.00 shot, not a 1.30 favourite.

How fast are sportsbook withdrawals in NZ?

Crypto cleared in four to twelve hours at the better books, e-wallets in one to two working days, cards in three to five. Gunsbet has no crypto cashier, so its realistic floor is the e-wallet timeline.

Two delays specific to betting: a bet is not a withdrawable balance until the market settles (some props settle days later), and integrity reviews on unusual betting patterns are more common here than at casino sites. See fast payout casinos for our full timing methodology.

Can I bet in New Zealand dollars?

At TAB NZ, obviously. At the offshore books it varies — most hold a NZD balance, but some price in euros and convert. That conversion costs 2–3% each way, which is larger than most bookmakers' margin and turns a competitively priced book into an expensive one.

Check the account currency before you deposit. Where an operator advertises in euros we convert at roughly 1.96 NZD per EUR and label it.

Do I pay tax on sports betting winnings in NZ?

No, as a recreational bettor. Inland Revenue treats betting winnings as the proceeds of chance rather than income, so there is nothing to declare or pay regardless of the amount. The narrow exception is betting carried on as a business with system and scale. See tax on gambling winnings in NZ.

Why do bookmakers limit winning accounts?

Because a book's business model is balancing liability at a margin, not competing with sharp customers. A customer who consistently beats the closing price costs the book money, so its risk team reduces the maximum stake it will accept — sometimes to a few dollars.

This is standard across the industry, rarely explained to the customer, and effectively unappealable. It is worth knowing in advance: if you become good at this, your reward is a restricted account rather than a bigger one.

Are multis worth betting?

Mathematically, no. The bookmaker's margin compounds with every leg. A four-leg multi where each leg carries 104% overround produces roughly 17% total margin against you, against about 4% on a single bet.

They are the most profitable product a sportsbook sells and the most heavily promoted, which are the same fact viewed from two sides. If you bet multis, do it knowing you are paying four times the margin for the entertainment of a bigger number.

What should I check before depositing at a sportsbook?

Five things. Currency — can you hold NZD, or are you paying an FX spread? Margin — sample three markets you would actually bet and calculate the overround. Withdrawal caps — in the terms, not from support. Bonus turnover — the multiple and the minimum odds that qualify. Market depth in your codes — a book with 40 NRL markets is different from one with four.

That takes about ten minutes and it is worth more than any review, including this one.

All of them run in a mobile browser without a download. Read the legal position before you open an offshore account.
