Debit and credit cards

Debit cards — the default, with one weakness

Visa and Mastercard debit are the most widely accepted methods at every site we cover. Deposits are instant and there is no operator fee. The weakness is the return journey: a card withdrawal takes two to five working days, and that is the card scheme's processing timeline, not the casino's. No operator can make a card refund faster.

If you deposit by card and expect to withdraw, plan for that delay or add an e-wallet at registration. Note also that some New Zealand banks apply restrictions to gambling merchant codes; a declined card deposit is usually your bank, not the casino.

Credit cards — don't

Why a credit card deposit costs far more than it looks

Your issuer almost always codes a gambling deposit as a cash advance, not a purchase. That means: no interest-free period, so interest accrues from the transaction date at typically 20%+ p.a.; a cash advance fee of about 2–3% on top; and no eligibility for rewards.

You also cannot usually withdraw winnings to a credit card — operators refund up to the original deposit and require a different method for anything above it.

Separately and more importantly: depositing borrowed money is the clearest behavioural warning sign in gambling. If that is where you are, the useful page is responsible gambling, not this one. Several New Zealand banks let you set a permanent gambling block on your card, and it takes two minutes to apply.

Prepaid vouchers

Neosurf and Paysafecard are prepaid vouchers you buy in advance and redeem at the cashier. They are deposit-only, which is their weakness and — used deliberately — their strength.

Why a voucher can be a good choice

  • A hard spending cap — you cannot deposit more than the voucher is worth
  • No card or bank details given to the operator
  • Nothing gambling-related appears on your bank statement beyond the voucher purchase
  • Instant deposits with no chargeback exposure

Why it cannot be your only method

  • You cannot withdraw to a voucher — ever
  • Closed-loop rules mean you must add and verify a second method before cashing out
  • A purchase fee applies at the retailer
  • Availability in New Zealand retail is patchier than in Europe

If you use a voucher as a budgeting tool — which is a genuinely sensible reason to use one — add a withdrawal-capable method at registration, not when you have a balance to move.

Paysafecard casino NZ

Paysafecard is a prepaid voucher you buy with cash or card and redeem with a 16-digit PIN. It is accepted at some — not all — of the Curaçao-licensed sites serving New Zealand, and availability at New Zealand retail is patchier than in Europe.

It has the same shape as Neosurf: excellent as a hard spending cap, impossible as a withdrawal route. You cannot be charged more than the voucher is worth, no card or bank details reach the operator, and nothing gambling-related touches your bank statement beyond the voucher purchase. But it is deposit-only, so you need a second, withdrawal-capable method verified on the account.

If a prepaid voucher is your preference, Neosurf is more widely accepted at the sites we list. Use either as a budgeting tool, never as your only method.

Can you use PayPal at online casinos NZ players join?

Generally no, and it is worth explaining why rather than just listing it as unavailable.

PayPal works with gambling merchants only where it has approved them, and that approval is largely restricted to operators licensed in markets PayPal is comfortable with — the UK, parts of Europe, regulated US states. The offshore Curaçao, Anjouan and Tobique licensees serving New Zealand today do not generally meet that bar, so PayPal is absent from their cashiers.

If what you want is the PayPal shape — a layer between your bank and the operator, so no card details reach the casino — the practical substitutes here are Skrill, Neteller and MiFinity. They behave the same way, appear as a wallet top-up on your statement, and pay out in one to two working days.

This may change. A domestically licensed New Zealand market from 2027 is exactly the kind of regime that brings mainstream payment providers with it.

Which casinos accept Apple Pay NZ side? Very few of the offshore sites serving New Zealand, for the same reason as PayPal: both sit behind approval processes that favour domestically licensed operators.

Where they do appear, understand what they are. Apple Pay and Google Pay are wrappers around your underlying card, not separate payment methods. Every card consideration still applies — the closed-loop rule, the two-to-five-day withdrawal timeline, and critically the cash advance problem if the card behind the wallet is a credit card.

The convenience is real — no typing card numbers, biometric confirmation — but it changes nothing about speed or cost.

Bank transfer and POLi

Bank transfer

Slow in both directions, occasionally charged, and the method most likely to be queried by your bank. One to two days in, three to seven days out, sometimes NZ$15–25 in fees. Use it only when nothing else is available, or for a large withdrawal where the fee is proportionally small and you do not mind waiting.

POLi — still running in New Zealand

POLi lets you pay straight from internet banking with no card. Australia Post closed the Australian service in September 2023; the New Zealand one continues under Merco Ltd, with the major banks supported. It is deposit-only, which is the only thing that should shape how you use it.

If you like paying from internet banking, POLi is a perfectly reasonable deposit route. Just make sure a withdrawal-capable method is verified on the account at the same time, because your winnings cannot come back this way.

Methods to avoid, and why

Negative recommendations are more useful than positive ones, and almost nobody publishes them.

  1. Credit cards

    Cash advance coding means interest from day one plus a 2–3% fee, and you cannot withdraw winnings to them. There is no scenario where this is the right choice.

  2. POLi as a primary method

    Progressively blocked by New Zealand banks and deposit-only regardless.

  3. Any voucher as your only method

    Guarantees a delay on your first withdrawal while you add and verify an alternative.

  4. A currency you do not hold

    An account denominated in euros costs you 2–3% each way in conversion. On a NZ$2,000 cycle that is roughly NZ$100 — more than any fee in this market.

  5. An exchange deposit address as a crypto withdrawal destination

    Some exchange addresses rotate or are shared, and funds arriving from a gambling service can be flagged under the exchange's own policies. Withdraw to a wallet you control, then move it on.

The closed-loop rule, explained

Nearly every licensed operator enforces the rule that money returns by the route it arrived. It exists for anti-money-laundering reasons: allowing funds in by one method and out by another is a classic laundering pattern, so regulators require the loop to close.

What it means in practice:

  • Deposit by Visa, withdraw to that same Visa — usually up to the deposited amount, with any excess paid by another verified method.
  • Deposit by Skrill, withdraw to Skrill.
  • Deposit by Neosurf or POLi, and you cannot withdraw at all until you add and verify something else.
  • Mixing methods across multiple deposits often means the operator returns funds proportionally across them, which is slow and confusing.

The setup that avoids all of this

At registration, before you deposit anything: verify your identity, verify one deposit method, and verify one withdrawal-capable method. An e-wallet or a crypto address works well for the second. Ten minutes once, and the single most common cause of withdrawal delay never applies to you.

Verifying your payment method, and casino withdrawal to bank account NZ

Every licensed operator must verify the payment instruments on your account. Expect to provide:

  • For a card — a photo showing the card front with the middle eight digits covered and your name visible, plus the back with the CVV covered.
  • For an e-wallet — a screenshot of the account overview showing your name and the account or email ID.
  • For crypto — confirmation of the wallet address, sometimes with a signed message or a small test transaction.
  • For bank transfer — a bank statement header showing your name, address and account number.

Alongside these you will need photo ID (passport or New Zealand driver licence) and proof of address dated within three months. Upload all of it on the day you register. An account verified upfront pays out on the operator's stated timeline; one verified at withdrawal adds one to three days to every figure on this site.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best payment method for NZ online casinos?

It depends on which half of the transaction you care about. For depositing, a Visa or Mastercard debit card is simplest and instant. For withdrawing, crypto is dramatically faster — we measured a median of 9 hours against 3.1 working days for cards.

The best overall setup for most Kiwi players is an e-wallet such as Skrill, Neteller or MiFinity: instant deposits, one to two day withdrawals, and it keeps gambling transactions off your bank statement, which matters to some people. If speed is the priority, USDT on the Tron network is the fastest and cheapest option available.

Can I use POLi at NZ online casinos?

Yes. This is widely misreported, so to be clear: Australia Post closed POLi in Australia in September 2023, but the New Zealand service continues under local ownership (Merco Ltd). ANZ, ASB, BNZ, Kiwibank, TSB and Westpac are supported, though individual banks periodically change their stance and some discourage it, because POLi works by taking your internet banking credentials.

The real catch is structural: POLi is deposit-only, so you can never withdraw to it. Use it to fund an account if you like it, but always have a withdrawal-capable method — a debit card, e-wallet or crypto address — verified alongside it.

Should I use a credit card to deposit at a casino?

No, and the reason is financial rather than moral. Your card issuer almost always codes a gambling deposit as a cash advance rather than a purchase. A cash advance has no interest-free period, so interest begins accruing the moment the transaction posts, typically at 20%+ p.a., and there is usually a fee of 2–3% on top.

You also generally cannot withdraw winnings to a credit card — most operators will only refund up to the original deposit amount. And depositing on credit is the clearest single indicator that a session has stopped being recreational; if that describes you, our responsible gambling page is the more useful one.

What is a closed loop payment rule?

The rule, enforced at nearly every licensed operator, that money must return by the route it arrived. Deposit by Visa, withdraw to Visa. Deposit by Skrill, withdraw to Skrill.

It exists for anti-money-laundering reasons and it catches people constantly, because several popular New Zealand deposit methods cannot receive a withdrawal at all — Neosurf, POLi and Paysafecard among them. If you deposit by one of those, your first withdrawal will stall while you add and verify a second method, at exactly the moment you least want a delay.

Do NZ banks block casino transactions?

Sometimes, and it varies by bank and over time. New Zealand banks are not obliged to block offshore gambling transactions, but some apply their own restrictions to merchant category code 7995 (betting and casino). A card deposit that worked last month can be declined this month without notice.

If a deposit is declined, the practical options are an e-wallet (which appears on your statement as a wallet top-up rather than a gambling transaction) or crypto (which removes the bank from the transaction entirely). Some banks also offer a customer-controlled gambling block, which is a genuinely useful tool if you want one.

Are there fees on casino deposits and withdrawals in NZ?

Operator fees are rare at the sites we cover — none of our top five charges on crypto or e-wallets. Bank transfer sometimes attracts NZ$15–25.

The cost that actually bites is currency conversion. If the site holds your balance in euros rather than New Zealand dollars, you pay a spread of roughly 2–3% converting in and the same converting out. On a NZ$2,000 deposit and withdrawal cycle that is around NZ$100, which dwarfs any flat fee. Check the account currency before you deposit.
