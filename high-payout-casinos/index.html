What "high payout casino" actually means

The phrase gets used loosely enough to be meaningless, so here is the definition we use and rank against.

A high payout casino is one where the average return to player across the games you will realistically play is above the market norm, and the terms do not claw that advantage back. Three separate components, all of which have to hold:

  1. The catalogue's average RTP

    Not the best title in the lobby — the average across what is actually promoted and played. A site with one 99% game buried on page nine and a front page of 94% branded titles is not a high payout casino.

  2. The deployed configuration

    Studios ship the same game at several RTP levels and operators choose. Two sites can both carry Gorilla Mayhem and run it at 96.5% and 94.0% respectively. We check the info panel in each lobby rather than quoting the studio's headline figure.

  3. The terms that cap conversion

    A maximum conversion, a withdrawal cap or a heavy game weighting can make a high-RTP catalogue pay worse in practice than a lower one. RTP is theoretical; the cashier is real.

The arithmetic, once

RTP applies to turnover, not to deposits. If you deposit NZ$100 and play it through ten times — which happens quickly at NZ$0.40 a spin — your turnover is NZ$1,000. At 96% RTP your expected loss is NZ$40, or 40% of your deposit. At 94% it is NZ$60. That two-point difference is 50% more expensive for the identical session, and it is why catalogue RTP matters more than most players assume.

The trap in that table

Notice that the best-RTP games are exactly the ones weighted at 0–10% for bonus wagering. That is deliberate. If you claim a NZ$100 bonus at 40x and try to clear it on blackjack weighted at 10%, your NZ$4,000 requirement becomes NZ$40,000 of turnover. Clearing a bonus and maximising RTP are opposing goals — pick one per session and know which you are doing.

The comparison that actually matters in New Zealand

Forget which offshore casino is 0.4 points better than another for a moment. The consequential number is the gap between online and the machines down the road.

Class 4 gaming machines — pub and club pokies under the Gambling Act 2003 — return roughly 85–90%. Online pokies at the sites above return 95–97%. On identical turnover, the pub machine costs you around three times as much.

The reason is structural. A Class 4 machine funds a venue, a machine lease, staffing and a mandated community grant stream from the same turnover. An online operator carries none of those costs and competes in a market where RTP is published and directly comparable. Neither arrangement is a scandal — the Class 4 surplus goes to community grants by design — but a player should know which one buys more playing time.

Getting the best return in practice

  1. Decide whether this is a bonus session or an RTP session

    They are incompatible. Clearing a bonus means playing 100%-weighted pokies at capped stakes. Maximising return means playing blackjack or high-RTP pokies with no bonus attached. Mixing them produces the worst of both.

  2. Check the info panel, every time

    Ten seconds. The deployed RTP is frequently not the one in the review, and it can change between visits when an operator swaps configuration.

  3. Prefer European or French roulette over American

    Same game, 97.3% or 98.65% versus 94.74%. The double-zero wheel exists purely to take more, and it sits in most lobbies next to the single-zero one at the same table limits.

  4. Learn blackjack basic strategy if you play blackjack

    It is a printable chart, it takes an evening, and it is worth several percentage points of RTP. Playing blackjack on instinct gives away most of the advantage that makes it the best game in the lobby.

  5. Skip progressives unless you accept the trade

    Base RTP of 88–92% is the price of the jackpot. Fine as an occasional small-budget flutter, poor as a main game.

  6. Read the conversion cap before you deposit

    A high-RTP catalogue behind a 3× conversion cap pays worse than a mediocre one without. The cap is in the terms; find it.

Frequently asked questions

Spinjo is close behind with a broader library including the full NetEnt high-RTP range.

Note that "highest payout" is a property of the games and the terms, not a marketing claim. Any site can advertise "98% payout"; what matters is the RTP of the titles you will actually play and whether a conversion cap limits what you can withdraw.

What does RTP actually mean?

Return to player: the proportion of all money staked on a game that the game returns as winnings, calculated over millions of spins. A 96% pokie returns NZ$96 for every NZ$100 staked across the entire player base over its lifetime — not to you, and not in your session.

The complement is the house edge. 96% RTP means a 4% house edge. That 4% is what the operator keeps, and it is applied to turnover rather than to deposits, which is why a NZ$100 bankroll can produce NZ$1,000 of turnover and NZ$40 of expected loss.

Which casino games have the highest RTP?

Table games, comfortably. Blackjack played with correct basic strategy runs above 99% RTP. Baccarat's banker bet is about 98.9%. French roulette with la partage is 98.65%, against American double-zero roulette at 94.74%.

Pokies sit lower, typically 94–97%, with outliers such as Book of 99 at 99% and Blood Suckers at 98%. The catch: the high-RTP table games are precisely the ones excluded or heavily discounted in bonus wagering, because the operator's edge is so thin. That is not a coincidence.

Does a high RTP mean I will win?

No, and this is the most important thing on this page. Every RTP under 100% is a mathematical guarantee of loss over a long enough run. RTP tells you which loss is smaller, not how to avoid one.

It also says nothing about a single session. A 98% pokie can take your entire bankroll in twenty minutes, and a 94% one can pay 500× on your third spin. RTP is a long-run average with enormous short-run variance around it. Choose high RTP because it makes your entertainment cheaper, not because it makes you a winner.

How can I check a game's RTP myself?

Open the game in the lobby, click the information or paytable icon (usually an 'i' or a hamburger menu), and look for "Return to Player" or "Theoretical RTP". That figure is the configuration actually running on your spins.

This matters because many studios ship a title in several RTP versions — 96.5%, 95%, 94% — and the operator chooses which to deploy. A review quoting 96.5% may be describing a different deployment from the one in front of you. Always check the panel, not the review.

Do high payout casinos have worse bonuses?

Often, and for a structural reason: a site running a higher-RTP catalogue keeps less per dollar of turnover, so it has less to spend on acquisition. The sites with 777% and 1000% headlines are generally not the ones with the best returns.

Which matters more depends entirely on volume. If you deposit once and play for a weekend, the bonus is worth more. If you play regularly across a year, catalogue RTP is worth far more — a two-point RTP difference on NZ$20,000 of annual turnover is NZ$400, which exceeds most welcome offers.

What is a conversion cap and how does it hurt payout?

A maximum conversion limits how much of your bonus-derived winnings can become withdrawable cash, often expressed as a multiple of your deposit — "maximum conversion 5x deposit" against a NZ$50 deposit caps you at NZ$250 no matter what you win with the bonus.

This is why a headline RTP figure can be meaningless. A 97% catalogue with a 3x conversion cap pays worse in practice than a 95% catalogue with none. We check the caps on every site and factor them into the ranking above.

Are online casino payout percentages audited?

The games are, at reputable operators. Studios such as NetEnt, Pragmatic Play, Play'n GO and Evolution certify their RNGs and RTP figures through independent labs — eCOGRA, iTech Labs and GLI are the main ones — and those certifications are a condition of the studio licensing its games to an operator.

What is less commonly audited at Curaçao-licensed sites is the operator-level monthly payout report that some regulated markets require. Treat a site's own published "98.2% payout last month" figure with scepticism unless an independent auditor is named.

Do high payout casinos pay out faster?

They are separate things entirely. Payout percentage is about game returns; payout speed is about the cashier. A site can have an excellent catalogue and a three-day withdrawal queue — Roby is close to that description.

Kingdom happens to be strong at both, which is why it tops this page and features on our fast payout casinos page as well. If speed is your priority rather than returns, start there instead.

Is blackjack really better than pokies for payout?

Mathematically, yes, and by a wide margin. Blackjack with correct basic strategy is above 99% RTP against a typical pokie's 96%. On NZ$1,000 of turnover that is roughly NZ$10 of expected loss versus NZ$40.

Three caveats. You must actually play correct basic strategy — casual play runs several points worse. Table game turnover accumulates far more slowly than pokie spins, which changes the comparison. And blackjack is usually weighted at 10% or 0% towards bonus wagering, so if you are clearing a bonus, the maths inverts entirely.

What RTP do New Zealand pub pokies have by comparison?

Class 4 gaming machines in New Zealand pubs and clubs run at roughly 85–90% return to player under the Gambling Act 2003 framework. That is 6 to 11 percentage points below a typical online pokie.

In cash terms: NZ$1,000 of turnover costs about NZ$130 at a pub machine and about NZ$40 at a 96% online pokie. The surplus from Class 4 machines funds community grants, which is the policy reason the return is set where it is — but it does mean the same money buys you far less playing time in a pub than on a phone.

Should I chase a progressive jackpot for the payout?

Understand what you are buying. A slice of every stake on a progressive network funds the jackpot pool, so the base game RTP is materially lower — often 88–92% excluding the jackpot contribution. The headline RTP including the jackpot may look normal, but you only realise that portion if you win a prize almost nobody wins.

If you want the best returns, avoid progressives. If you want a lottery ticket attached to a pokie and you are content that it is one, play them with a small fixed budget and no expectations.

Can a casino change the RTP on a slot?

Not spin to spin, and not in response to how you are playing — but the answer is more nuanced than a flat no, and this is the detail most pages get wrong.

Studios frequently ship the same title in several RTP configurations: a game marketed at 96.5% may also exist at 95%, 94% and sometimes lower. The operator chooses which version to deploy in its lobby, and it can switch that deployment.

What it cannot do is alter the outcome of a certified game while you play it, or tune the return to your individual account. The house edge is a property of the paytable, verified in certification by labs such as eCOGRA and iTech Labs.

The practical consequence: check the in-game information panel rather than a review, because the number there is the configuration running on your spins, and it can differ between two casinos carrying the same game.

How do you calculate RTP?

RTP is total winnings returned divided by total amount staked, expressed as a percentage and calculated by the studio over millions of simulated rounds — not something you can measure in a session.

What you can calculate is what it costs you. Expected loss = turnover × (100% − RTP). So NZ$1,000 of turnover on a 96% pokie is NZ$1,000 × 4% = NZ$40. On a 98% title it is NZ$20.

The number people get wrong is turnover, not RTP. Turnover is everything you stake, not what you deposit — a NZ$100 bankroll played through ten times is NZ$1,000 of turnover, which is why a 2-point RTP difference matters far more than it first appears.
