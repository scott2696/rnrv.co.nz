The testing process, step by step

Before any operator appears anywhere on RNRV, it goes through this. It takes roughly three weeks.

  1. Register as an ordinary customer

    A reviewer opens an account in their own name, from a New Zealand IP address, with no affiliate account, no VIP contact and no advance notice to the operator. We record every friction point in the sign-up flow, because that is the experience a reader will have.

  2. Complete KYC

    Photo ID, proof of address, proof of payment method, submitted on day one. We record how long verification takes and how clearly the requirements are communicated, because that is the single largest determinant of how fast a first withdrawal goes.

  3. Deposit real New Zealand dollars

    Our own money, on at least two different payment rails where the operator supports more than one. We record deposit speed, any currency conversion applied, and whether the advertised minimum matches the actual minimum.

  4. Play the catalogue

    A minimum of 200 real-money pokie spins or 100 live dealer hands. During this we sample 60 titles from the lobby and read each one's RTP from its in-game information panel — not from the studio's documentation, because operators deploy different configurations.

  5. Read the complete terms

    Our editor reads the full terms and conditions and the bonus policy, recording the wagering multiple, what it applies to, the maximum bet during wagering, game weighting, expiry, maximum conversion, withdrawal caps and fees. Not the summary on the promotions tile — the actual terms.

  6. Test support with a real question

    A live chat asking something that requires an actual answer, usually about withdrawal caps or bonus weighting. We record response time and whether the agent answered or deflected. Transcripts are kept.

  7. Request a withdrawal and time it

    To the minute, with two separate timestamps: when the operator approved, and when funds were available. We repeat this at different times of day and on different rails. Ninety-four such withdrawals underpin the current site.

  8. Score, fact-check, publish

    The reviewer scores against the weights below. A second person fact-checks every claim. The page is published with a byline and a review date. No operator sees it first.

Hard gates, and how to check if a casino is licensed NZ side

Some things are not weighted. They are pass or fail, and a failure caps where an operator can rank regardless of everything else.

  • An unverifiable licence caps an operator below first place. MadCasino scores well on offer size and would otherwise rank higher; it does not publish its licence, so it cannot top a ranking on this site.
  • An unpublished wagering requirement scores zero on the bonus criterion. Not an estimated value — zero. If a reader cannot price the offer, neither can we, and we will not guess on an operator's behalf.
  • A refused legitimate withdrawal removes an operator entirely. No score, no listing, no exceptions.
  • No responsible gambling tools removes an operator entirely. Deposit limits, loss limits, time-outs and self-exclusion must exist in account settings.
  • Any marketing that could target under-18s removes an operator entirely.

The test we apply to ourselves

It should not, and it does not. Our two lowest-paying operators are Spino (25%) and the TechOptions group sites including IviBet, HellSpin and SlotsGem (20%). Spino ranks second on our fast payout page and carries the only 0x-wagering offer we recommend.

What we refuse to do

  • Countdown timers, fake scarcity or manufactured urgency
  • Claiming to have tested something we have not
  • Publishing a bonus figure we cannot verify in the operator's own terms

Editorial standards

Named authorship and expertise

Every page is written by one of three named reviewers, each with a defined specialism and relevant background. Credentials are on the authors page. We do not publish anonymous content.

Separation of fact and assessment

Where we state something as measured — a withdrawal time, an overround, an RTP — it was measured by us and we say when. Where we are giving a view, it reads as a view. Where an operator's claim is unverified, we attribute it to the operator rather than adopting it.

Primary sources for legal and tax claims

Every legal, regulatory and tax statement on this site is traceable to legislation, a government publication or published professional analysis, cited on the page. Where the Department of Internal Affairs or Inland Revenue is the authoritative source, we say so and link to it.

Dated review cycles

Bonus terms monthly. Payout timings quarterly, with fresh withdrawals. Legal pages monthly while the licensing regime is in transition. Every page shows its last review date and reviewer.

Responsible gambling is not a footer afterthought

Every page carries helpline information. Our rankings deduct for operators lacking deposit limits, loss limits, time-outs and self-exclusion. We publish the arithmetic showing that expected value is negative, including on pages where that is commercially inconvenient — the RTP comparison and the no-deposit valuation are both examples.

Corrections, complaints and challenges

If we have got something wrong

Tell us through the contact page. We correct the page, note the correction and its date at the foot, and update the review date. We do not silently edit.

The most useful reader reports are about things that change between our test cycles: a withdrawal that took far longer than our published figure, a bonus term that has been amended, a payment method that has stopped working. Those surface changes we would otherwise catch a quarter late.

If you have a dispute with an operator

We cannot intervene on your behalf — we are a comparison site, not a regulator or an ombudsman, and we have no authority over any operator. What we can do is point you at the escalation path that works, which is set out in full on our complaints section. And we do want to hear about it, because a pattern of credible unresolved complaints is grounds for removing an operator from this site.

If you are an operator

You are welcome to correct a factual error, and we will fix it and note the correction. You cannot review a page before publication, request a ranking change, or purchase placement. Contact details are on the contact page.

Frequently asked questions

Do casinos pay RNRV for a good review?

No. No operator on this site has paid for placement, none has seen a page before publication, and none has editorial input of any kind.

We do earn affiliate commission when a reader opens an account through one of our links, which is disclosed on every page carrying one. The commission rate is not an input to the score. If it were, the rankings would look different — Roby and Spino pay us the least of any operator here and both rank ahead of higher-paying sites, and MadCasino pays well and is ranked third with a published warning about its licence.

How do you actually test a casino?

A reviewer opens an account in their own name, completes KYC like any other customer, deposits their own New Zealand dollars, plays a minimum of 200 real-money spins or 100 live hands, requests a withdrawal and times it to the minute. Our editor separately reads the complete terms and conditions. Someone else opens a live chat with a specific, genuine question and records the response.

That takes about three weeks per site. It is why we list twelve casinos rather than fifty.

Why do you list sites you have criticised?

Because omitting them would be less useful, not more. MadCasino has the largest headline bonus available to New Zealanders and readers search for it. We can either not cover it, and leave people to find it through a site that will not mention the missing licence — or cover it, rank it on its merits, and publish the warning prominently.

We think the second is more useful. The same applies to Roby, whose licence is not clearly published and whose withdrawals took three days in our testing. Both appear with those facts attached in every table they appear in.

How often do you update rankings?

Bonus terms are re-verified monthly, because they change most often. Payout timings are re-measured each quarter with fresh withdrawals. Legal and regulatory pages are re-checked monthly against Department of Internal Affairs publications, which matters particularly while the licensing regime is in transition.

Every page carries the date it was last reviewed and by whom, at the bottom. If a page has not been checked recently, you will be able to see that.

What would make you remove a casino from the site?

Any of the following: a refusal to pay a legitimate verified withdrawal; a licence that we cannot verify where one was previously claimed; a pattern of credible unresolved complaints on independent forums; terms changed retrospectively to a player's detriment; or marketing that targets under-18s.

We have removed operators from consideration during testing for two of these. We publish the fact of a removal but not always the detailed reasons, because an unproven public allegation about a specific company is a different thing from a review.

Are the ratings out of 5 comparable across pages?

Yes. A single score per operator is produced from the weighted criteria below and used consistently across the site. What changes by page is the ordering: our fast payout page ranks on measured withdrawal time, and our high payout page ranks on catalogue RTP, so the same operator can sit in a different position while carrying the same rating.

We think that is more honest than publishing a different score per page to justify a different order.

Who writes the content on this site?

Three named reviewers, each with a defined area — Claire Morrison on testing and payments, Elizabeth King on regulation and bonus terms, Claire Morrison on sports and racing. Every page carries a byline, the author's credentials are on the authors page, and every page is fact-checked by a second person before publication.

We do not publish anonymous content, and we do not publish operator-supplied copy.

How do you handle corrections?

Directly and visibly. If we get something wrong, we correct the page and note the correction and its date at the foot of it. We do not quietly edit a page and leave the review date unchanged.

If you have found an error — a bonus term that has changed, a payout time that no longer matches your experience, a legal claim that is out of date — tell us through the contact page. Reader reports about changed withdrawal times are among the most useful things we receive, because they surface changes between our quarterly re-tests.

How to choose an online casino NZ: what should I actually check?

Six things, in about ten minutes. Licence — a reference that resolves on the regulator's own register, not a footer badge. Legal entity named with an address. Withdrawal caps findable in the terms. Wagering requirement published on the offer. Responsible gambling tools in account settings. Live chat that answers a specific question with a specific answer.

Then test the thing that matters: deposit small, complete KYC, and request a withdrawal before you play seriously.

How to tell if an online casino is legit

Work from evidence rather than impression. A legitimate operator publishes a verifiable licence, names its company, states bonus terms in full including the maximum bet and conversion cap, and offers deposit limits and self-exclusion.

The clearest negative signals: a licence badge with no number, wagering requirements published nowhere, terms reserving the right to void winnings at "sole discretion" without cause, and support that answers a withdrawal question by offering another bonus. Two operators we list fail on the first two, and we say so on every page they appear on.

What makes an online casino safe?

Verifiable licensing, a named legal entity, published caps and terms, certified games from recognised studios, and working responsible gambling tools. Those are the checkable parts.

The part no offshore operator currently offers New Zealanders is fund segregation — Curaçao, Anjouan and Tobique do not require player money to be held separately from operating money. That is why the practical safety rule here is to hold only your playing balance on site and withdraw the rest.

How do I check if a casino is licensed in NZ?

Today, no casino is licensed in New Zealand — no DIA licence has been issued yet. So the check is: which offshore authority, and does the reference resolve on that authority's own register?
