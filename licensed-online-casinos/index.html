Is online gambling legal in NZ? The position in one paragraph

The short version

You are not breaking the law. It has never been an offence for a New Zealander to play at an offshore online casino, and the Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 did not change that — it regulates operators, not players. What is changing is which operators may lawfully serve you. From 1 December 2026, an operator needs a New Zealand licence or a pending application to continue. Fifteen licences exist, allocated by auction. Some sites you use today will exit the market. Withdraw your balances rather than leaving them.

What the Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 actually does

The Act creates New Zealand's first dedicated licensing regime for online casino gambling. Three features matter most.

It has extraterritorial reach

The Act applies to any operator enabling online casino gambling from New Zealand, wherever that operator is incorporated. An operator in Curaçao, Anjouan or Malta that accepts New Zealand customers is within its scope. This is the provision that makes the regime enforceable against a market that is almost entirely offshore.

It caps the number of licences

Fifteen licences, allocated through a competitive auction rather than granted on application to anyone meeting a standard. Each covers a single brand; no applicant may hold more than three; each runs up to three years with a possible five-year renewal.

It restricts advertising severely

Only licensed operators may advertise online casino gambling to New Zealanders, and even then several whole categories of advertising are prohibited. Because no licences have been issued yet, all online casino advertising to New Zealanders has been barred since 1 May 2026.

It is supported by two instruments: the Online Casino Gambling Regulations 2026 (in force 3 July 2026), whose Part 4 contains the advertising rules, and the Online Casino Gambling (Minimum Standards) 2026 (in force 8 July 2026), which set out the operational requirements a licensee must meet.

How many online casino licences NZ will issue: the fifteen explained

How many, and for how long

Fifteen licences. Each valid for up to three years, with the possibility of renewal for a further five. Each covers a single brand.

How they are allocated

By competitive auction, run through GETS. Operators first submitted an expression of interest; accepted EOIs proceeded to the auction; successful bidders may then apply for a licence.

Concentration limit

No single applicant may be granted more than three licences. That prevents one group taking the market, though fifteen brands could still mean as few as five operators.

What an application requires

A business plan, plus detailed strategies covering advertising and marketing, consumer protection, harm prevention and minimisation, and regulatory compliance. Assessed together with the EOI material to determine suitability.

The practical consequence of a capped, auctioned market is concentration. A fifteen-brand market is a very different landscape from the effectively unlimited Curaçao licensing that characterises the operators on this site today. Whether that produces better consumer outcomes or merely fewer choices is the open question in New Zealand gambling policy.

Advertising restrictions — among the tightest in the region

Section 10 of the Act permits an online casino gambling advertisement directed at New Zealanders only if it is published in accordance with a licence, and the gambling is not substantially similar to, likely to compete with, or involve betting on a protected New Zealand lottery.

Because no licences have been issued, operators have been barred from advertising online casino gambling to New Zealand customers since 1 May 2026.

  • Broadcasting
  • Video on-demand content

Restrictions on those permitted methods

  • No advertising on public transport
  • No advertising on the front page of a multi-page publication

Categories prohibited outright

  • Advertisements causing offence
  • Advertisements referring to community contributions
  • Advertisements breaching regulation 44 (age requirements) or regulation 45 (harm minimisation messages)
  • Advertisements involving other types of gambling

Enforcement so far

The DIA has not waited for the licensing regime to become operational before acting.

It has issued infringement notices to multiple social media influencers who promoted offshore casino services to New Zealand audiences, and to the offshore operator itself. Three features of that enforcement are worth noting because they indicate how the regime will be applied:

  • The DIA treated each advertisement as a separate contravention, so one influencer accumulated repeated penalties across several posts.
  • It maintained ongoing monitoring of the offending accounts rather than acting once.
  • It directed social media platforms and internet service providers to take down the illegal advertising.

That is an active regulator with practical tools, and it is a material change from the pre-2026 position in which offshore operators marketed into New Zealand with little friction.

What regulated online casinos in New Zealand mean for you as a player

  1. You are not committing an offence

    There is no penalty in New Zealand law for a player who gambles at an offshore online casino. The Act creates obligations for operators and for advertisers. No provision creates a player offence, and none has been proposed.

  2. Some sites will disappear from the New Zealand market

    Fifteen licences against a market currently served by hundreds of offshore brands means most will exit. An operator that has not applied must cease by 1 December 2026.

  3. Withdraw your balances

    The single most practical step. Do not leave money in an account at an operator that may stop serving New Zealand. Treat a casino account as a wallet for the session, not a place to store funds.

  4. Complete your verification now

    If a site exits and you need to withdraw quickly, an unverified account is the worst possible position to be in. Upload ID, proof of address and proof of payment method at every site where you hold funds.

  5. Expect better protection eventually

    The Minimum Standards and the consumer-protection requirements in licence applications should deliver player protections the offshore regime does not offer — most importantly around fund protection. That is a 2027 proposition, not a current one.

  6. Keep records

    Deposits, withdrawals, balances and account correspondence. If an operator exits messily, contemporaneous records are the only useful thing you will have.

Which online casinos are licensed in NZ right now?

As at September 2026: none

No operator currently holds a New Zealand online casino licence, because none has been issued yet. Any site claiming to be a "DIA licensed online casino" or a "licensed online casino NZ" today is either misusing the phrase or describing an offshore licence from Curaçao, Anjouan or Tobique.

That is not a scandal — the regime simply has not reached the issuing stage. Expressions of interest closed 14 August 2026, the auction determines who may apply, and applications open for successful bidders in October 2026. The first genuine New Zealand licence holders will appear after that.

When licences are granted we will publish the New Zealand online casino licence holders list here and on our new casinos NZ page, updated as each is announced. Until then, treat "licensed" claims the way you would treat any other marketing: ask which authority, ask for the reference number, and check it on that authority's own register.

How to check if a casino is licensed

  1. Find the licence statement

    It should be in the footer, naming an authority and a reference. If there is only a badge image and no number, there is nothing to check.

  2. Identify the authority

    Today that will be Curaçao Gaming Control Board, Curaçao eGaming, Anjouan Gaming Authority or Tobique Gaming Commission — not the DIA.

  3. Verify on the regulator's own register

    Not on the casino's site. Spinjo's OGL/2023/176/0095 resolves on the Curaçao register; MadCasino publishes nothing to check.

  4. Confirm the legal entity

    A named company — Hollycorn N.V., Dama N.V., TechOptions Group B.V. — with a registered address. No entity, no accountability.

Do I pay tax on casino winnings NZ? Is gambling tax free in New Zealand?

Yes, for recreational players, and the new licensing regime does not change it. You do not pay tax on casino winnings in NZ. Inland Revenue treats gambling winnings as the proceeds of chance rather than income, so there is no rate, no threshold, no withholding and nothing to declare — whatever the amount.

Two exceptions: gambling carried on as a business, and separately the disposal of cryptocurrency you won, because Inland Revenue treats cryptoassets as property. Both are set out in full on our tax on gambling winnings NZ page.

Frequently asked questions

Is online gambling legal in New Zealand?

For players, yes. It has never been an offence for a person in New Zealand to gamble at an online casino hosted overseas, and the Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 did not create one. The Act regulates operators. There is no penalty in New Zealand law for a Kiwi who deposits at an offshore casino site.

For operators the position has changed substantially. From 1 December 2026, only an operator holding a New Zealand licence — or with a licence application under consideration — may continue to offer online casino gambling to New Zealanders.

What is the Online Casino Gambling Act 2026?

New Zealand's first dedicated licensing regime for online casino gambling. It came into force on 1 May 2026 and it has extraterritorial reach, applying to any operator enabling online casino play from New Zealand regardless of where that operator is incorporated.

It is supported by two instruments: the Online Casino Gambling Regulations 2026, in force 3 July 2026, which include the Part 4 advertising rules; and the Online Casino Gambling (Minimum Standards) 2026, in force 8 July 2026, which set out what a licensed operator must meet.

How many online casino licences will New Zealand issue?

Fifteen. Each licence covers a single brand, each runs for up to three years with the possibility of a further five-year renewal, and no single applicant may hold more than three.

That is a deliberately small market. Fifteen brands, with a maximum of three per operator group, means at least five separate operators but potentially a concentrated field. Compare that to the effectively unlimited licensing in Curaçao that characterises the current market.

What happens on 1 December 2026?

It is the hard transition date. From that day, an operator that has neither been granted a licence nor has an application under consideration must cease conducting online casino gambling in New Zealand.

Operators with a pending application may continue to operate — but without advertising — until a decision is made. If the application is declined, the operator must cease operations and exit the market.

Practically: some sites New Zealanders use today will stop serving New Zealand. Withdraw balances rather than leaving money in an account you may lose access to.

What are the penalties for operating without a licence?

An operator that operates or advertises without a licence after 1 December 2026 faces a civil penalty of up to NZ$5 million for a body corporate or NZ$300,000 for an individual. The Department of Internal Affairs can also take other enforcement measures and issue formal warnings.

These penalties apply to operators and to those who advertise on their behalf. They do not apply to players.

Can online casinos advertise in New Zealand?

Not currently, and only in a tightly restricted way once licences are issued. Because no licences have yet been granted, operators have been barred from advertising online casino gambling to New Zealand customers since 1 May 2026.

When licensed operators can advertise, Part 4 of the Online Casino Gambling Regulations 2026 applies. Some methods are permitted — broadcast, video on demand, physical publication, direct communication, online and in-software. But several categories are prohibited outright, including advertisements using sponsorships, endorsements or affiliate arrangements; advertisements using inducements or interactive features; those promoting impulsivity or urgency; personalised advertisements; and those that are false or misleading. There are also placement restrictions — no advertising during a live broadcast or within 30 minutes either side, none on public transport, none on a publication's front page.

Has the DIA enforced the new rules yet?

Yes. The Department of Internal Affairs has issued infringement notices to multiple social media influencers who promoted offshore casino services to New Zealand audiences, and to the offshore operator itself.

Notably, the DIA treated each advertisement as a separate contravention — one influencer accumulated repeated penalties across several posts — maintained ongoing monitoring of the offending accounts, and directed social media platforms and internet service providers to take down the illegal advertising. That is an active enforcement posture, not a paper regime.

Where does sports betting fit in?

Somewhere entirely different, and confusing the two is the most common error on this subject.

Under the Racing Industry Amendment Act 2025 (Royal Assent 27 June 2025), TAB NZ is the only operator legally permitted to offer racing and sports betting to New Zealanders. Betting is closing to offshore operators at the same time as online casino is opening to licensed ones. See our online betting NZ page.

What is the legal gambling age in New Zealand?

It depends on the product. 20 for New Zealand's land-based casinos. 18 for Lotto, Class 4 gaming machines (pub pokies), TAB betting and — under the new online casino framework — online casino gambling. Offshore operators almost universally apply 18 as well.

Every legitimate operator verifies date of birth at KYC. An underage account is closed and any winnings voided.

Will player protections improve under the new regime?

That is the intention. The Minimum Standards 2026 set out requirements licensed operators must meet, and licence applications require detailed strategies on consumer protection, harm prevention and minimisation, and regulatory compliance.

The most consequential change from a player's perspective would be fund protection. Offshore Curaçao, Anjouan and Tobique licences do not require operators to segregate customer money from operating money, which means your balance is an unsecured claim if an operator fails. A domestic regime with meaningful standards should improve that materially. Until it is operational, the practical rule stands: hold only your playing balance on site.

Do I pay tax on online casino winnings in New Zealand?

No, as a recreational player. Inland Revenue treats gambling winnings as the proceeds of chance rather than income, so there is nothing to declare and nothing to pay. This is unaffected by the new licensing regime.

The exceptions are the professional gambler, and separately the disposal of cryptocurrency won at a casino, since Inland Revenue treats cryptoassets as property. See tax on gambling winnings in NZ.

What should I do with my existing casino accounts?

Four practical steps. Do not leave balances sitting — withdraw winnings promptly, particularly as 1 December approaches. Complete KYC now at any site where you hold funds, so a withdrawal cannot stall on verification at the worst moment. Keep records of deposits, withdrawals and balances. And watch for operator communications about New Zealand market status, which will be your first signal that a site is exiting.

None of this is alarmist. It is the ordinary prudence appropriate to a market in transition.

Is online poker legal in New Zealand?

For you as a player, yes — on the same basis as online casino. New Zealand law does not make it an offence to gamble at a site hosted overseas, and the Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 regulates operators rather than players.

On the operator side, the Act's definition of online casino gambling is what determines whether a poker room needs one of the 15 licences to keep serving New Zealanders after 1 December 2026. Peer-to-peer poker, where the operator takes rake rather than banking the game, sits in a less settled position than house-banked casino games, and the DIA's guidance is the authoritative source as the regime beds in.

Worth noting for tax: poker is also the game most likely to raise the professional gambler question, because it is played against other players rather than the house and a genuine long-run edge is possible.

Are there legal online casinos NZ players can use today?

Not in the sense of New Zealand-licensed, because no NZ online casino licence has been issued yet. Every site accepting New Zealanders today holds an offshore licence.

Legal online casino NZ 2027 is the realistic timeframe: applications open for successful bidders in October 2026 and the regulated regime is expected operational during 2027. Until then, "legal" accurately describes your position as a player, not the operator's licensing status.

When do online casinos become legal in NZ?

They have never been illegal for you. For operators, the milestones are: applications open October 2026, unlicensed operators must cease 1 December 2026, and the fully regulated online casinos New Zealand regime is expected to be operational during 2027.

So the honest answer is that online casinos become licensed in New Zealand during 2027, having never been unlawful for players to use.

Is it legal to gamble online in New Zealand?

Yes. There is no offence and no penalty for a New Zealander who gambles at an online casino hosted overseas, and none has been proposed. The Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 creates obligations for operators and advertisers, not for players.

Sports and racing betting differs: TAB NZ holds the only lawful right to take a New Zealand bet, but again the offence sits with an offshore operator accepting it, not with you.

What is the Department of Internal Affairs online casino licence process?

Three stages. Operators submitted an expression of interest through the Government Electronic Tenders Service (closed 14 August 2026); accepted EOIs proceed to a competitive auction; successful bidders may then apply for a licence from October 2026.

A DIA licensed online casino application requires a business plan plus strategies on advertising, consumer protection, harm prevention and regulatory compliance. Fifteen NZ online casino licence grants are available, each covering one brand, with no applicant holding more than three.

What happens to unlicensed online casinos NZ players use?

From 1 December 2026 an unlicensed operator with no pending application must cease serving New Zealand. One with an application under consideration may continue without advertising until it is determined; if declined, it must exit immediately.

Penalties for operating unlicensed after that date reach NZ$5 million for a body corporate and NZ$300,000 for an individual. Withdraw balances rather than leaving them — see offshore casinos NZ legal status.

Will there be a New Zealand online casino licence list?

Yes, once licences are granted. The DIA will publish the holders, and we will maintain an NZ online casino licence holders list here and on our new casinos NZ page as each is announced.

Until then, treat any site describing itself as a licensed online casino NZ operator as referring to an offshore licence. Ask which authority and which reference number, then check it on that authority's own register.
