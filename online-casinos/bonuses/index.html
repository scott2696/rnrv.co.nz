How to read that table

"Expected cost to clear" is the statistically expected loss you incur generating the required turnover on 96% RTP pokies. It is not a fee and it is not guaranteed — you might clear a bonus in profit, or lose your whole balance before completing it. It is the honest average, and it is the number the industry never publishes.

The insight it gives you: on Smash, you receive NZ$600 in bonus funds and it costs roughly NZ$280 in expected losses to convert it. That is genuine positive expected value if you were going to play anyway. On Kingdom, you receive NZ$600 and it costs roughly NZ$720 to convert — the offer is worth less than it costs. That distinction is invisible from the headline percentage, and it is the entire point of this page.

The four terms that actually decide a bonus's value

  1. The wagering multiple — and what it applies to

    40x on the bonus is a NZ$4,000 requirement per NZ$100 of bonus. 40x on deposit plus bonus is NZ$8,000 for the same offer. Always read the preposition. Sites frequently write "40x the bonus amount" and sites frequently write "40x the sum of deposit and bonus", and the difference doubles your cost.

  2. The maximum bet while wagering

    Usually NZ$5–8 a spin. Breaching it once — including via autoplay you configured earlier, or a bonus-buy purchase — lets the operator void the bonus and all winnings from it. This is the single most common reason a cleared bonus is refused, and it is entirely avoidable.

  3. Game weighting

    Pokies 100%, live blackjack and roulette 10% or 0%, jackpot and bonus-buy titles often excluded. If you play table games, a bonus is not a bonus.

  4. Maximum conversion

    The ceiling on how much bonus winnings become cash — often 3–10× your deposit. This is what makes enormous headline numbers meaningless and it is usually in the bonus policy rather than on the offer itself.

Types of casino bonus, and which are worth having

Welcome / deposit match

A percentage of your first deposit as bonus funds, often spread over several deposits. The standard offer. Value depends entirely on wagering and conversion cap. Worth it when the wagering is under about 35×.

Free spins

Spins on a named pokie. Wagering is usually applied to the winnings rather than the spin value, with a conversion cap. Better value attached to a deposit than as a no-deposit offer, because the caps are higher.

No deposit

Credit or spins before you fund an account. Always high wagering and a low cap — Lucky7even's 20 spins carry 50×. A free look at the site, not a route to cash. See no deposit bonuses.

Reload

A smaller match on subsequent deposits, often weekly. Lower percentage but frequently lower wagering too. Often better value per dollar than the welcome offer.

Cashback

A percentage of net losses returned, sometimes with no wagering at all. Rivo's 25% VIP cashback is the standout here. The best ongoing offer type for a regular player, because it repeats.

VIP / loyalty

Tiered rewards for volume — faster withdrawals, higher caps, a dedicated manager, better cashback. Genuinely valuable at the higher tiers, but reaching them requires turnover that costs more than the rewards return.

When you should decline the bonus

Nobody else in this market writes this section, which is why it is here.

Claim it when

  • You play pokies, which are weighted at 100%
  • You were going to deposit anyway
  • The wagering multiple is under about 35x and is published
  • You have read the max bet and set your stake below it
  • The expiry window is realistic at your normal stakes
  • The conversion cap is absent or generous

Decline it when

  • You play live dealer or table games — weighting makes clearing impossible
  • You want to withdraw quickly; a bonus locks your deposit too
  • The wagering requirement is not published
  • You are depositing a large sum you may want back
  • The expiry is three days and you play twice a week
  • The conversion cap is 3x deposit or lower

Most operators let you decline at the cashier or opt out in account settings. If you cannot find how, contact support before depositing — a bonus applied automatically to a deposit you wanted unencumbered is a genuine problem to unwind afterwards.

Six traps that void an otherwise cleared bonus

  1. A single spin over the max bet

    Including one from autoplay set earlier in the session. Set your stake at the start of a bonus session and leave it alone.

  2. Buying a bonus feature

    Most operators exclude bonus-buy purchases from wagering, and some treat a purchase with bonus funds as a breach that voids the offer entirely.

  3. Playing an excluded game

    Jackpot pokies and certain high-RTP titles are frequently excluded. The excluded list is in the terms and it is worth a two-minute read.

  4. Requesting a withdrawal mid-wagering

    At most sites this forfeits the bonus and every dollar won from it. The cashier will often let you submit the request without warning you clearly.

  5. Missing the promo code

    Bet&Play's sports offer requires code SPORT at deposit. A code missed at deposit generally cannot be applied afterwards.

  6. Depositing with an excluded method

    Skrill and Neteller are excluded from bonus eligibility at a surprising number of operators. Check the eligible methods list before you fund the account, not after.

Frequently asked questions

Spino is arguably better again for a crypto player, because its headline welcome package carries 0x wagering — anything you win is withdrawable immediately. It is smaller and crypto-only, but 0x is close to unheard of and it is the only offer on this site with no clearing cost at all.

What does wagering requirement mean?

The multiple of your bonus you must stake before bonus funds become withdrawable cash. At 40x on a NZ$100 bonus, you must place NZ$4,000 of bets.

The critical detail most pages skip: check whether the multiple applies to the bonus alone or to deposit plus bonus. A 40x requirement on a NZ$100 deposit plus NZ$100 bonus is NZ$8,000 of turnover, not NZ$4,000 — double the cost for an identical-looking offer. Smash's 10x applies to deposit plus bonus, which is why its effective figure is 20x rather than 10x, and still far below the market.

What is a fair wagering requirement in New Zealand?

The market norm for NZ-facing sites is 35–40x on the bonus. Use this as a scale:

  • 0x — exceptional. Spino is the only one here.
  • 10–20x — genuinely good. Smash and Rivo.
  • 30–35x — reasonable. Kingdom at 30x, Roby at 35x.
  • 40–50x — market standard to steep. Spinjo and CrownSlots at ~40x, Bet&Play's casino side at 50x.
  • Over 50x, or unstated — treat as having no assessable value. MadCasino does not publish a figure.
Why do bonuses have a maximum bet limit?

To stop you converting a bonus into a withdrawable balance in a handful of large bets. Typical caps run NZ$5–8 per spin while a bonus is active.

This is the term that most often costs Kiwi players a cleared bonus. A single spin above the cap — including one triggered by autoplay at a stake you set earlier, or a bonus-buy purchase that exceeds it — lets the operator void the bonus and every dollar won from it. Not reduce: void. Set your stake deliberately at the start of a bonus session and do not change it.

What is game weighting?

How much each game contributes to your wagering requirement. Pokies almost always count 100%. Live blackjack and roulette typically count 10% or 0%. Jackpot pokies and bonus-buy titles are often excluded entirely.

The effect is large. A NZ$4,000 requirement on blackjack at 10% weighting means NZ$40,000 of actual turnover. The weighting table is in the terms, and it is worth more attention than the headline percentage.

What is a maximum conversion cap?

A ceiling on how much bonus-derived winnings can become withdrawable cash, often expressed as a multiple of your deposit — "maximum conversion 5x deposit" against a NZ$50 deposit caps you at NZ$250 regardless of what you win.

This is the term that makes a large headline meaningless. A 777% bonus with a 3x conversion cap is worth less than a 100% bonus without one. Find the cap before you claim; it is usually in the bonus policy rather than on the offer tile.

Should I always claim the welcome bonus?

No, and this is the most useful thing on the page. Declining a bonus is often the better financial decision.

Claim it if you play pokies, you were going to deposit anyway, the wagering is under about 35x, and you have read the max bet and conversion cap. Decline it if you play live dealer or table games (weighted at 10%, so clearing is near-impossible), if you want to withdraw quickly, if the wagering is unstated, or if you are depositing a large sum — because a bonus locks your own deposit until the requirement is met.

A bonus you cannot clear is not a gift. It is a restriction on money that is already yours.

What types of casino bonus are there?

Welcome / deposit match — a percentage of your deposit as bonus funds. The standard first offer. Free spins — spins on a named pokie, with wagering usually applied to the winnings. No deposit — credit or spins before you fund an account, always with high wagering and a low cap. Reload — a smaller match on later deposits. Cashback — a percentage of net losses returned, sometimes with no wagering, which makes it one of the better ongoing offers. VIP and loyalty — tiered rewards for volume.

Cashback deserves particular attention: Rivo's 25% VIP cashback is worth more to a regular player than most welcome offers, because it applies repeatedly rather than once.

How long do I have to clear a bonus?

Typically 7 to 30 days. Shorter windows are more common on larger offers, which is not a coincidence — a NZ$19,500 package with a 7-day window is not realistically clearable by a normal player, and the expiry is part of the design.

Lucky Vibe's bonuses expire in three days, which is the tightest we found. Work out before you claim whether the required turnover is achievable in the window at the stakes you actually play. If it is not, the offer is decorative.

Do I have to pay tax on bonus winnings in New Zealand?

No. Inland Revenue treats gambling winnings as the proceeds of chance rather than income, and that includes winnings derived from bonus funds. There is nothing to declare and nothing to pay for a recreational player.

The exceptions are the professional gambler and, separately, the disposal of cryptocurrency won as a bonus — see tax on gambling winnings and the crypto tax section.

Can I withdraw my deposit if I have an active bonus?

Usually only by forfeiting the bonus. Most operators lock deposit and bonus together while wagering is incomplete, so a withdrawal request either gets blocked or voids the bonus and any winnings from it.

Some sites separate the two and let you withdraw your own deposit while losing only the bonus. Which arrangement applies is in the terms, and it is worth knowing before you claim rather than after.

Are big bonus percentages a red flag?

Not automatically, but they shift where you should look. A 777% or 1000% headline is funded from somewhere, and that somewhere is usually a combination of high wagering, a tight conversion cap and a short expiry.

The reliable test: can you calculate what the offer costs to clear? Rivo's 1000% at 10x is calculable and genuinely good. MadCasino's 777% at unstated wagering is not calculable and we treat it as having no assessable value. The problem is never the size of the number; it is the absence of the terms that let you price it.

What is a 40x wagering requirement?

It means you must stake 40 times the bonus before bonus funds convert to withdrawable cash. On a NZ$100 bonus that is NZ$4,000 of turnover.

Two details change the answer materially. First, check whether the multiple applies to the bonus alone or to deposit plus bonus — the second doubles it, so 40x on a NZ$100 deposit plus NZ$100 bonus is NZ$8,000. Second, check game weighting: at 10% weighting on table games, that NZ$4,000 becomes NZ$40,000.

For what each multiple costs in practice, see the table below. For context, 35–40x is the New Zealand market norm, and anything at or under 20x is genuinely good.

How do you calculate a wagering requirement?

Turnover required = bonus × multiple (or deposit plus bonus × multiple, where the terms say so).

Then work out what generating that turnover costs: expected cost = turnover × (100% − RTP). On 96% pokies that is 4% of turnover.

Worked example on a NZ$100 deposit with a NZ$600 bonus at 10x on deposit plus bonus: (100 + 600) × 10 = NZ$7,000 turnover, at an expected cost of NZ$7,000 × 4% = about NZ$280 to convert NZ$600 of bonus funds. That is positive expected value if you were going to play anyway.

Run the same sum at 40x on the bonus alone and you get NZ$24,000 of turnover costing about NZ$960 — more than the bonus is worth. That single calculation is the most useful thing on this page.

How do you beat wagering requirements?

You do not, and pages promising otherwise are describing either bonus abuse, which gets accounts closed and winnings voided, or simple arithmetic dressed up as a trick.

What you can do is choose requirements worth accepting. Pick low multiples — Smash and Rivo at 10x, Spino at 0x — over big headline percentages. Clear on 100%-weighted pokies, never on table games at 10%. Stay under the maximum bet, because one spin over it voids everything. Pick low-volatility titles, since you need turnover rather than variance and a high-volatility game will often wipe the balance before the requirement is met.

And genuinely consider declining. A bonus locks your own deposit until the requirement is met, so an unclearable one is a restriction rather than a gift. See when to decline a bonus.
