## 2. What the best competitors do well

### 2.1 H3 depth is the structural differentiator
The pages that win break every concept into a named sub-section. `covers.com/casino/canada` runs **46 H3s**; `cardplayer.com/au` runs **49**. `casino.org/en-au/` runs 11 and reads as thin by comparison.

H3 depth is not decoration — it is how a page captures long-tail and "how do I…" queries without writing separate pages. Each named H3 is a snippet candidate.

### 2.2 First-person, verifiable experience
`covers.com` is the clearest case. It runs sections titled **"My favourite online casino in Canada this week"**, **"This week's play test"**, and narrative H3s like *"Five hours later, I was $200 ahead"*. `casino.org/new-zealand/` runs **"My pokie of the month"** and **"Latest playtest"**.

This is the Experience leg of E-E-A-T executed literally, and it is the single hardest thing for a competitor to copy because it requires actually doing the thing.

### 2.3 Negative recommendation content
`casino.org/new-zealand/` runs **"Kiwis should avoid these casinos"** and **"Report a casino"**. `covers.com` (US) runs **"Risks of illegal offshore casinos"** and **"Why you should never use an offshore online casino"**.

Telling readers what *not* to do is a strong trust signal and almost nobody in the NZ market does it.

### 2.4 Genuine data tables
`covers.com/canada` carries **11 tables**; `racingpost.com` carries 9; `casino.org/new-zealand/` carries 10. These win definition and comparison snippets.

`bookies.com/nz` and `casinomeister.com/nz` carry **zero tables** despite 19k and 27k words respectively. That is a large amount of content earning no featured-snippet real estate.

### 2.5 Explicit trust sections
`covers.com` runs a dedicated **"Why trust Covers?"** H2 in both markets. `casinomeister.com` runs **"Why Trust *Our* Casino Reviews?"** and **"How Do We Write Our Casino Reviews?"**. `casino.org` runs **"Sources and references"** — the only competitor in the set citing primary sources.

### 2.6 Localisation that goes beyond a currency symbol
`covers.com/canada` gives every **province and territory its own H3** (13 of them). `covers.com` (US) organises around **state-by-state legality**. `cardplayer.com/au` has a dedicated section on **the ACMA's role** and the IGA 2001.

The NZ pages do far less of this, which is the most exploitable weakness in the set.

### 2.7 Conversion patterns used repeatedly across all five markets
- Sticky or repeated ranked operator list with logo, rating, bonus, one-line USP, CTA
- Category-split "best for X" H2s (bonuses, pokies, live, mobile, payout)
- A comparison table immediately after the top list
- Author byline with photo near the top
- "Last updated" date, frequently with the month named in the H1 or H2 ("Top-rated for September")
- FAQ accordion at the foot with FAQPage schema
- Responsible gambling block immediately before the footer

### 3.2 Structural weaknesses in the ranking NZ pages

- **`gambling.com/nz/online-casinos`** — category-split H2s with almost no H3 depth, a thin 6-question FAQ, and no measured data. It ranks on domain authority, not page quality. **Most beatable page in the top 3.**
- **`bookies.com/nz`** — 46 H3s but **zero tables** and no first-person testing. Volume without evidence.
- **`casinomeister.com/nz`** — 27k words, zero tables, and its ranked list (All British Casino, Bet365, Betsafe, Bitstarz, Casino Euro, Casino Friday) reads as a legacy list rather than a current NZ one.
- **`pokernews.com/nz`** — its FAQ H3s bleed into site navigation ("Company", "Who We Are", "Social"), which is a templating failure that dilutes the page.
- **Every NZ competitor** ranks operators on bonus size and asserts payout speed without evidence.

### 3.3 The credibility gap across the whole set
Not one competitor publishes:
- The number of withdrawals behind a payout claim
- Timestamps, or approval-vs-settlement as separate figures
- Commission rates alongside rankings
- A hard gate that caps an operator's rank regardless of other scores

## 7. Honest risk note

Two findings from this research have commercial implications the client should be aware of, because they were not in the brief:

1. **Part 4 of the Online Casino Gambling Regulations 2026 prohibits advertising to New Zealanders that uses "sponsorships, endorsements, or affiliate arrangements."** Since 1 May 2026 all online casino advertising to New Zealanders has been barred pending licence issuance. The DIA has already issued infringement notices to influencers and an offshore operator, treating each post as a separate contravention, and has directed platforms and ISPs to remove listings.

2. **Under the Racing Industry Amendment Act 2025, it is an offence for an offshore bookmaker to accept a bet from a New Zealander.** TAB NZ holds the sole lawful right. The sportsbooks in the operator list sit outside that framework.

The site has been written so that both positions are stated plainly on the relevant pages rather than omitted. That is the right call for E-E-A-T regardless — but the underlying regulatory exposure is a business question worth taking New Zealand legal advice on before scaling promotion of this site.
